The Menongue Project · Angola

Skills, steady work, and health for the women and families of Menongue.

Deja General is a community organization in southern Angola that turns proven, simple models — trade training, classes, and small local businesses — into lasting opportunity.

Support the work See our programs

Why Menongue

Hardworking people, very few ways up.

Menongue is a small city in Angola's Cuando province, near the southern border with Namibia. Angola is rich in natural resources, but most families see little of that wealth. Outside of government jobs, steady income is hard to find — and women and young children carry the heaviest share of that instability.

What we saw again and again: people with a trade — seamstresses, carpenters, welders — tend to have more stable homes, become local leaders, and help make their neighborhoods safer. Simple classes like English and self-reliance bring people together and widen what they can do.

So our model is simple: replicate and scale business and class models that already work, in communities like Menongue, with a special focus on helping women build more secure lives.

What we do

Four programs under one roof

Each program stands on its own, and together they're designed to be modular and easy to replicate in new locations.

Flagship

Women's Health Classes

Practical classes that promote the health and safety of women — and, through them, stronger families. More than 300 women take part today, and we're adding participant surveys and expert-reviewed curriculum in 2027.

Sewing Shop

Young women learn to sew from established local tradeswomen, then move straight into paid production work. The shop already fills contracts for school uniforms, guard uniforms, and other goods.

English Classes

English courses run by capable local teachers, built to open doors — including pathways into programs like BYU–Pathway Worldwide for continued education.

Moto-Taxi Fleet

A small fleet of motorcycle taxis gives drivers a daily income and helps fund the organization. Drivers wear Deja-branded helmets and vests — so every ride is also a rolling advertisement.

Our story

It started in Menongue.

Founder Jack Haney first came to Angola as a missionary and was sent to Menongue. Living there showed him what textbooks and headlines couldn't — both the hardship families face and the difference a trade, a class, or a steady job can make.

In summer 2026 our team returned, rented and refurbished a building, bought sewing machines and motorcycles, furnished classrooms, and hired a local team. Day to day, Deja General is run by Angolans, led by our director, Adivini Bastos.

“I don't really know, bro, but I do know that my God is the author of the impossible. I think it's all gonna work out.” — Adivini Bastos, Director, when asked how he was holding up
